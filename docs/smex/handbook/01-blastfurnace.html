The Blast Furnace
The blast furnace is the heart of the operation: a tall multiblock built mostly from refractory bricks, with an iron door, two tuyeres (air intakes) low on the shaft and two gas outlets high up. Place the door block and shift + rightmouse it to see the build outline, then fill in every highlighted block until the structure reports complete.

Blocks required:
- 98 x Refractory Bricks (any tier)
- 1 x Blast Furnace Door (the control block, placed first)
- 2 x Tuyere
- 2 x Molten Metal Tap
- 2 x Gas Outlet (any refractory tier)
- 1 x Reinforced Hopper
- 1 x Bell Hopper
The hollow shaft inside is left empty - the hoppers fill it with blast mix.

Feeding it. The furnace does not eat ore directly! It burns Blast Mix, a packed pile of ore and fuel. You cannot craft blast mix by hand - it is produced by the pair of hoppers stacked above the shaft. The Reinforced Hopper is the magazine: open it (rightmouse) and load three things - crushed iron ore, fuel, and lime (flux). Crush iron ore in a pulverizer, coke is baked from coal in a coke oven. Scrap iron bits are not furnace feed - they go to the Bessemer converter, which remelts them. The hopper combines 12 crushed iron + 1 lime + one batch of fuel into 16 blast mix and stockpiles it internally.

Coke or charcoal. The fuel slot takes either, and the hopper counts them by carbon rather than by the piece: 2 coke make a batch, and 4 charcoal do the same work. The two mix freely within one batch - 1 coke and 2 charcoal is also a batch - and coke is always spent first. Charcoal keeps a furnace fed while the coke ovens catch up, at twice the volume to haul.

Below it sits the Bell Hopper, which drips the stockpiled blast mix down into the shaft. Toggle dropping with ctrl + rightmouse on either of the hoppers. The dropping stops on its own once the furnace is full.

Fuel pile broken? Blast mix behaves just like a coal pile: rightmouse the ground to lay loose mix back down, stack more onto it, and feed it back to the furnace through hoppers. It even burns as a normal fuel in a pinch, but don't expect much from it.

Firing it. Let the bell hopper fill the furnace, after which ignite the blast mix inside, and close the door. Once every pile in the hearth is burning, the door is shut and the exhaust outlets are not choked, the furnace climbs toward ~1543 °C on plain cold air - comfortably past iron's melting point of ~1482 °C - and starts turning blast mix into a reservoir of molten iron and molten slag as soon as it gets there.

How fast it melts is set by two things at once, and the panel shows both. The first is how far the hearth is over the melting point: cold blast leaves a thin margin and melts slowly, while the preheated air described in the Hot Blast article lifts the ceiling to 1740 °C and melts about three times faster. The second is air. A hot hearth asks for more blast than a cool one, and if the blowers cannot deliver what it asks for it melts in proportion to what arrives - so Blast: 24 of 40 needed on the panel means the furnace is running at little over half speed and wants another blower. With no air at all it melts nothing, however hot it looks.

A lit furnace runs until you stop it - there is no clock on the campaign. What does put it out is opening the door, letting exhaust back down the tuyeres, or robbing the hearth below 144 blast mix; the molten iron it was holding then solidifies in the hearth as blocks you must mine out.

Two things stall it rather than kill it. A backed-up exhaust drops it to its cold-blast ceiling and halts the melt until the flue is clear, which is what lets you swap between two cowper stoves without losing the campaign. A full molten reservoir does the same - tap it and the melt resumes on the next tick. Neither wastes any charge, and blast mix is never spoiled: a pile lit outside a working furnace burns down and goes cold, still blast mix, ready to light again.
