The Blast Furnace
The blast furnace is the heart of the operation: a tall multiblock built mostly from refractory bricks, with an iron door, two tuyeres (air intakes) low on the shaft and two gas outlets high up. Place the door block and shift + rightmouse it to see the build outline, then fill in every highlighted block until the structure reports complete.

Blocks required:
- 98 x Refractory Bricks (any tier)
- 1 x Blast Furnace Door (the control block, placed first)
- 2 x Tuyere
- 2 x Molten Metal Tap
- 2 x Gas Outlet (any refractory tier)
- 1 x Reinforced Hopper
- 1 x Bell Hopper
The hollow shaft inside is left empty - the hoppers fill it with blast mix.

Feeding it. The furnace does not eat ore directly! It burns Blast Mix, a packed pile of ore and fuel. You cannot craft blast mix by hand - it is produced by the pair of hoppers stacked above the shaft. The Reinforced Hopper is the magazine: open it (rightmouse) and load three things - crushed iron ore, fuel, and lime (flux). Crush iron ore in a pulverizer, coke is baked from coal in a coke oven, and even scrap iron bits can be crushed back into crushed iron for resmelting. The hopper combines 12 crushed iron + 1 lime + one batch of fuel into 16 blast mix and stockpiles it internally (total 60 units of iron).

Coke or charcoal. The fuel slot takes either, and the hopper counts them by carbon rather than by the piece: 2 coke make a batch, and 4 charcoal do the same work. The two mix freely within one batch - 1 coke and 2 charcoal is also a batch - and coke is always spent first. Charcoal keeps a furnace fed while the coke ovens catch up, at twice the volume to haul.

Below it sits the Bell Hopper, which drips the stockpiled blast mix down into the shaft. Toggle dropping with ctrl + rightmouse on either of the hoppers. The dropping stops on its own once the furnace is full.

Fuel pile broken? Blast mix behaves just like a coal pile: rightmouse the ground to lay loose mix back down, stack more onto it, and feed it back to the furnace through hoppers. It even burns as a normal fuel in a pinch, but don't expect much from it.

Firing it. Let the bell hopper fill the furnace, after which ignite the blast mix inside, and close the door. Once every pile in the hearth is burning, the door is shut and the exhaust outlets are not choked, the furnace begins firing and climbs toward ~1400 °C on its own, but you need to hold it above iron's melting point (~1482 °C, see the Hot Blast article) for a few minutes and it enters the Melting phase, converting blast mix into a reservoir of molten iron and molten slag.

The fire is fragile! Opening the door, running out of blast mix, or letting the molten reservoir overflow will all snuff it out - and an extinguished melt solidifies in the hearth as iron blocks you must mine out. A backed-up exhaust does not kill the fire, it stalls it: the furnace falls back to its natural draught, stops melting and says so on the panel, then picks up again once the flue is clear - which is what lets you swap between two cowper stoves without losing the campaign. Keep the exhaust flowing and the door shut while it works the iron.
